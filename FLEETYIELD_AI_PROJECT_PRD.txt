1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM DEFINITION
================================================================================

1.1. Context & Industry Background:
In intercity bus passenger transport in India, thousands of private fleet operators
compete across fragmented corridors. Most operators manage pricing manually using
inflexible static fare tables or ad-hoc intuition. As departure approaches:
  - Off-peak buses travel half-empty (sub-40% occupancy), permanently losing perish-
    able seat capacity.
  - High-demand weekend or holiday departures sell out too quickly at base fares,
    forfeiting commercial yield that could offset operating diesel costs.
  - Operators distrust automated "black box" revenue management algorithms that
    propose drastic fare swings, violate route regulatory brackets, or execute
    unsupervised passenger-facing price changes.

1.2. Product Solution:
FleetYield AI is a transparent, explainable, human-in-the-loop dynamic yield management
and decision-support platform designed for TBS FleetOS. The platform combines real-
time trip occupancy tracking, rule-based demand classification, bounded fare recommen-
dations, explicit data-evidence quality scoring, and a fixed-fare baseline simulator.

1.3. Key Value Proposition:
  - Explainable: Every recommended fare explains its inputs, math, rules, and limits.
  - Air-Gapped & Safe: No fare ever touches passenger ticketing without deliberate
    operator review, approval, or override.
  - Strict Bounds: Algorithmic output is mathematically clamped within pre-approved
    minimum (`min_fare`) and maximum (`max_fare`) boundaries, rounded to ₹10 intervals.
  - Evidence-Aware: Every recommendation evaluates data freshness and completeness,
    flagging "Strong", "Limited", or "Insufficient" evidence to prevent algorithmic
    hallucination on stale or corrupted fixtures.
  - Benchmark-Grounded: Built-in baseline simulator tests dynamic yield against a fixed
    fare benchmark across 4 market elasticity scenarios, explicitly flagging when
    dynamic discounting might underperform static baselines.

2. TARGET PERSONAS & USE CASES
================================================================================

2.1. Primary Personas:
1. Route Revenue Manager / Yield Analyst:
   - Goal: Optimize occupancy and revenue across high-frequency intercity routes.
   - Pain Point: Spends hours reviewing spreadsheets; lacks real-time visibility into
     which buses are lagging in booking velocity.
   - Workflow: Filters trips by departure date and route; reviews recommendations;
     examines evidence quality audit details; approves or overrides proposed fares.

2. Operations Dispatcher:
   - Goal: Maximize load factors on departures leaving in the next 12 to 48 hours.
   - Pain Point: Needs fast, uncluttered status indicators showing empty buses without
     complex statistical overhead.
   - Workflow: Identifies low-demand departures (<30% occupancy); verifies validation
     alerts; approves off-peak promotional yield fares to accelerate bookings.

3. Fleet Executive / Business Owner:
   - Goal: Protect brand trust and ensure pricing complies with regulatory guidelines.
   - Pain Point: Terrified that an algorithm will slash fares below fuel costs or spike
     fares during emergencies, triggering public backlash.
   - Workflow: Enforces hard minimum/maximum fare guardrails; reviews historical audit
     logs; evaluates baseline simulation to verify revenue gain hypotheses.

4. Internal Compliance & Audit Officer:
   - Goal: Verify fair business practices, non-discriminatory pricing, and decision trails.
   - Pain Point: Inability to audit why a ticket cost was modified in legacy systems.
   - Workflow: Queries immutable operator decision logs; reviews rationale strings,
     before-and-after deltas, and timestamped operator signatures.

3. PRODUCT VISION & GUIDING PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

1. Operator Supremacy (Human-in-the-Loop):
   The algorithm advises; the operator decides. Automated systems stage proposed
   fares but never publish without human approval.

2. Determinism over Black Boxes:
   Fares are calculated using transparent, auditable business logic. Every price change
   can be explained to a passenger or regulator in plain language.

3. Zero Toleration for Fake Data / Phantom Claims:
   All dashboard cards, charts, and metrics are computed dynamically from real SQLite
   database rows. No hardcoded metric strings or stubbed placeholder buttons.

4. Defensive Input Validation:
   Garbage in, safety out. Stale, missing, or inverted input values immediately trigger
   validation blocks rather than calculating spurious recommendations.

5. Realism in Revenue Simulations:
   Never promise guaranteed financial windfalls. The simulator accurately models price
   elasticity, enforces seat limits, and highlights conditions where dynamic pricing
   loses money compared to a fixed fare.

4. NON-NEGOTIABLE GOVERNANCE, TRUTHFULNESS & ETHICAL SAFEGUARDS
================================================================================

4.1. Synthetic Demo Data Disclosure:
All starter trip fixtures, operator bookings, and route histories provided in the
default seed database are explicitly labeled as SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA. The system does
not claim that seed data represents real-world passenger records or live telemetry.

4.2. Rule-Based Transparency:
The demand classifier and recommendation engine are implemented using deterministic,
configurable rule policies. The product explicitly disclaims being an unsupervised
deep-learning or black-box neural model.

4.3. Production Air-Gap:
This prototype operates as a decision-support environment. Fares approved in the UI
update the internal database decision state and trip records; they do not alter live
external passenger-facing booking channels unless connected to production TBS APIs.

4.4. Ethical & Non-Discriminatory Pricing:
Dynamic adjustments are driven strictly by capacity, time-to-departure, and route
booking pace. The system strictly forbids individual profiling, behavioral tracking,
surge gouging during emergencies, or discriminatory demographic parameters.

4.5. Complete Resource Disclosure:
All third-party libraries, CSS frameworks, icons, and fonts used in the project are
itemized and documented in `THIRD_PARTY_RESOURCES.md` under open-source licenses.

6. CORE FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS & FEATURE SPECIFICATIONS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1. Trip & Inventory Management Module
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Provide comprehensive operational visibility into active bus inventory.
- Features:
  * Real-time calculation of trip metrics:
    - `total_capacity` (total seats on bus, e.g., 40, 48, 54).
    - `seats_booked` (cumulative passenger reservations).
    - `available_seats = total_capacity - seats_booked`.
    - `occupancy_pct = (seats_booked / total_capacity) * 100` (rounded to 1 decimal).
  * Route metadata: Route code, origin, destination, bus type (AC Sleeper, Non-AC
    Seater, Volvo Multi-Axle, etc.), scheduled departure, scheduled arrival.
  * Pricing limits: `base_fare`, `current_fare`, `min_fare`, `max_fare`.
  * Status Lifecycle: Scheduled, In-Progress, Completed, Cancelled.
  * Search, Filter & Sort:
    - Filter by origin, destination, bus type, occupancy range, and date.
    - Sort ascending/descending by departure time, occupancy, and current fare.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2. Data Quality & Pre-Flight Validation Engine (`validation_service.py`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Inspect every trip record before calculating recommendations or running
  simulations to ensure algorithmic integrity.
- Verification Rules:
  * Rule V1 (Seat Consistency): `seats_booked >= 0` AND `seats_booked <= total_capacity`.
  * Rule V2 (Positive Capacity): `total_capacity > 0`.
  * Rule V3 (Boundary Integrity): `min_fare > 0`, `max_fare > 0`, `min_fare <= max_fare`.
  * Rule V4 (Current Fare Validity): `current_fare >= min_fare` AND `current_fare <= max_fare`.
  * Rule V5 (Data Freshness / Age): `(now - last_updated) <= 120 minutes`. If age
    exceeds 120 minutes, data is flagged as STALE.
  * Rule V6 (Chronological Integrity): `departure_time > now` for actionable trips.
- Error Handling:
  * When validation fails, system sets `validation_status = 'INVALID'` or `'STALE'`,
    records specific `validation_errors` array, and blocks automated fare adjustments.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3. Rule-Based Demand Classification Engine (`demand_classifier.py`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Classify passenger demand into discrete, explainable categories.
- Unified Configurable Thresholds:
  * LOW Demand: `occupancy_pct < 30.0%`
    - Rationale: Bus is running severely under capacity. Price reduction needed to
      accelerate bookings before departure.
  * MEDIUM Demand: `30.0% <= occupancy_pct <= 70.0%`
    - Rationale: Standard healthy booking pace. Maintain baseline pricing.
  * HIGH Demand: `occupancy_pct > 70.0%`
    - Rationale: Strong booking velocity and high fill rate. Premium yield capture
      can be applied to remaining scarce seats.
- Observation Window & Velocity:
  * Computes booking pace: `bookings_per_hour = recent_bookings / observation_hours`.
  * Observation Window: Timestamped span `[window_start, window_end]` (default 4.0h).
  * Time-to-Departure Factor: Captures urgency (`hours_to_departure`).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.4. Bounded Dynamic Pricing Policy Engine (`pricing_policy.py`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Calculate proposed fares based on demand category while strictly enforcing
  safety boundaries and currency rounding.
- Policy Rules:
  * Low Demand Adjustment: Base discount of -10% applied to current fare.
  * Medium Demand Adjustment: 0% adjustment (retain current/base fare).
  * High Demand Adjustment: Premium surcharge of +15% applied to base fare.
  * Proximity Multipliers: If departure is < 24 hours away and occupancy remains < 30%,
    additional promotional step is allowed, subject to minimum boundary.
- Safety & Boundary Clamping:
  * `proposed_fare = clamp(calculated_fare, trip.min_fare, trip.max_fare)`.
  * Nearest ₹10 Rounding: All proposed fares rounded commercially to the nearest 10
    (e.g., ₹847 -> ₹850, ₹844 -> ₹840) to mirror real intercity cash/online conventions.
  * Boundary Precedence: If rounded fare falls below `min_fare`, it is clamped up to
    `min_fare`. If it exceeds `max_fare`, it is clamped down to `max_fare`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.5. Explainable Pricing Confidence & Evidence Quality Framework
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Provide full visibility into the reliability of the underlying data and
  the exact step-by-step logic behind each recommendation.
- Three Evidence Quality Tiers:
  1. STRONG DATA:
     * Requirements: All required fields valid, freshness age <= 120 mins, departure
       in future, observation window >= 2.0 hours.
     * Confidence Score: 0.90 to 0.95.
     * Action: Recommendation fully supported for operator review.
  2. LIMITED DATA:
     * Requirements: Core inputs valid, but historical booking window is brief (< 2.0h)
       or velocity data is sparse.
     * Confidence Score: 0.70.
     * Action: Computable recommendation, but flagged with a caution banner prompting
       the operator to verify offline booking patterns.
  3. INSUFFICIENT DATA:
     * Requirements: Triggered when critical fields are missing, invalid, data age
       exceeds 120 minutes, or boundary rules fail.
     * Confidence Score: 0.00.
     * Action: Algorithmic pricing is BLOCKED. Recommendation status marked as
       `BLOCKED`. System safely retains the current fare and displays blocker reasons.
- 7-Point Explainability Audit Details:
  Every generated recommendation produces a structured audit record containing:
  1. Current Occupancy & Threshold Applied (<30% Low, 30-70% Medium, >70% High).
  2. Booking Pace & Timestamped Observation Window (`window_start`, `window_end`,
     `window_hours`, `bookings_per_hour`).
  3. Time Until Departure (`hours_to_departure` and human-readable string).
  4. Last Successful Data-Update Timestamp & data age in minutes.
  5. Missing, Stale, Invalid, or Inconsistent Inputs (with detailed issue strings).
  6. Applied Pricing Rules & Rationale Factors.
  7. Final Recommended Fare & Strict Boundary Clamping (`min_fare` to `max_fare`).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.6. Human-in-the-Loop Operator Decision Workflow (`trip_service.py`, `models.py`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Enforce strict human authorization before any fare modification is adopted.
- Decision States:
  * `PENDING`: Staged recommendation awaiting operator review.
  * `APPROVED`: Operator accepts proposed fare as recommended.
  * `REJECTED`: Operator dismisses proposed fare; current fare remains unchanged.
  * `OVERRIDDEN`: Operator manually enters a custom fare (validated against min/max).
- Workflow Requirements:
  * Decision Modal in UI with side-by-side comparison (Current Fare, Proposed Fare,
    Absolute Delta, Percentage Change, Rationale).
  * Mandatory Operator Note / Reason capture for overrides and rejections.
  * Prevention of duplicate decisions: Once a recommendation is decided, it cannot
    be double-approved; a new recommendation cycle must be initiated.
  * Atomicity: Database transaction updates recommendation state, records an
    `operator_decisions` entry, and conditionally updates `trips.current_fare`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.7. Fixed-Fare Baseline vs. Dynamic Yield Simulator (`simulation_service.py`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Provide an empirical, non-marketing comparison between static fixed pricing
  and dynamic pricing across entire fleet schedules.
- Mathematical Simulation Guarantees:
  * Identical Initial Conditions: Both scenarios start from identical trip states
    (`seats_booked`, `base_fare`, `available_seats`).
  * Integer Booking Clamps: Simulated incremental bookings calculated as
    `max(0, round(incremental_demand))` and strictly capped by `available_seats`.
  * Non-Negative Revenue: Under all elasticity inputs, simulated bookings and
    revenues are mathematically guaranteed >= 0.
  * Exact Recalculation: Final revenue equals `(initial_seats + simulated_seats) * fare`.
- 4 Pre-Configured Benchmark Scenarios:
  1. Balanced Market: Moderate elasticity (Price elasticity of demand = -0.8).
  2. Inelastic Market (Underperformance Scenario): Price elasticity = -0.2. Highlights
     case where discounting off-peak seats erodes ticket value without attracting
     sufficient volume (-1.9% dynamic yield drop).
  3. Elastic Backlash: High price sensitivity = -1.8. Surge pricing on high-demand
     trips drives passengers to competitors (-1.5% dynamic revenue drop).
  4. Peak Holiday Surge: Strong inelastic demand = -0.4 during festival rush. Captures
     significant legitimate yield lift (+11.1%).
- Explicit Underperformance Detection:
  * If `dynamic_total_revenue < fixed_total_revenue`, simulator flags
    `dynamic_underperforms_baseline = True` and displays an explicit explanatory alert
    detailing why dynamic adjustments underperformed the static baseline.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.8. Persistent Audit Logging & Compliance Ledger (`audit_service.py`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Deliver an immutable audit trail for governance, compliance, and dispute
  resolution.
- Audit Log Attributes:
  * `id`: Unique incremental audit identifier.
  * `trip_id` & `route_name`: Subject trip and corridor.
  * `operator_id` & `operator_name`: Identity of deciding officer.
  * `action`: 'APPROVED', 'REJECTED', 'OVERRIDDEN'.
  * `previous_fare`: Fare prior to decision.
  * `new_fare`: Fare adopted as a result of decision.
  * `fare_delta`: Absolute currency change (`new_fare - previous_fare`).
  * `reason_notes`: Justification provided by operator.
  * `created_at`: Exact ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
- User Experience:
  * Searchable table in UI with filtering by action, route, and date range.
  * Export-ready structure for operational compliance reviews.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.9. System Assumptions & Configuration Management (`Assumptions.tsx`, `config.py`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Purpose: Maintain full transparency of all system parameters and business logic.
- Disclosed Parameters:
  * Demand Occupancy Thresholds (Low <30%, Medium 30-70%, High >70%).
  * Yield Step Adjustments (-10% off-peak, 0% medium, +15% high).
  * Data Freshness Window (120 minutes maximum age).
  * Commercial Rounding Granularity (Nearest ₹10).
  * Elasticity Formulas and Simulation Assumptions.
  * Explicit Statement of Model Type (Deterministic Rule Policy, not ML).

7. REST API ENDPOINTS SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

All endpoints communicate using standard JSON payloads over HTTP:

7.1. Trip & Inventory Endpoints:
  - `GET /api/trips`
    * Description: Fetch all trips with real-time occupancy, fares, and current
      recommendation status.
    * Query Parameters: `origin`, `destination`, `bus_type`, `status`.
  - `GET /api/trips/{trip_id}`
    * Description: Retrieve detailed single trip record with full recommendation,
      evidence quality audit, and history.
  - `POST /api/trips`
    * Description: Create a new trip record in inventory.

7.2. Recommendation Endpoints:
  - `POST /api/recommendations/generate`
    * Description: Trigger recommendation generation for a trip or all scheduled trips.
    * Behavior: Validates inputs, evaluates evidence quality, calculates proposed
      fare, clamps to bounds, and stages recommendation as `PENDING` or `BLOCKED`.
  - `GET /api/recommendations`
    * Description: List all staged recommendations with filters by status.

7.3. Operator Decision & Audit Endpoints:
  - `POST /api/decisions`
    * Description: Record operator decision on a staged recommendation.
    * Payload:
      {
        "recommendation_id": 4,
        "trip_id": 2,
        "decision": "APPROVED" | "REJECTED" | "OVERRIDDEN",
        "override_fare": 950.0 (optional, required if OVERRIDDEN),
        "operator_id": "OP-042",
        "operator_name": "Senior Dispatcher",
        "reason": "High weekend travel surge confirmed via terminal counter"
      }
    * Response: Updated decision record and updated trip state.
  - `GET /api/audit-logs`
    * Description: Retrieve chronological audit ledger of all operator actions.
    * Query Parameters: `trip_id`, `action`, `limit`, `offset`.

7.4. Simulation & Analytics Endpoints:
  - `POST /api/simulation/compare`
    * Description: Execute side-by-side fixed-fare vs. dynamic yield comparison.
    * Payload:
      {
        "scenario": "balanced" | "inelastic" | "elastic_backlash" | "peak_holiday",
        "elasticity_factor": -0.8
      }
    * Response: Aggregate metrics (fixed revenue, dynamic revenue, uplift percentage,
      underperformance flag, detailed per-trip simulation arrays).
  - `GET /api/metrics/overview`
    * Description: Return fleet-wide summary metrics (total buses, average occupancy,
      pending recommendations, total revenue, yield health).
  - `GET /api/assumptions`
    * Description: Return dictionary of all active system thresholds and settings.
  - `POST /api/data/reset-demo`
    * Description: Re-seed database with clean synthetic demo dataset.

8. DATABASE SCHEMA & ENTITY RELATIONSHIPS
================================================================================

8.1. Table: `trips`
  - `id`: INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT
  - `route_code`: VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL
  - `origin`: VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL
  - `destination`: VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL
  - `bus_type`: VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL
  - `departure_time`: DATETIME NOT NULL
  - `arrival_time`: DATETIME NOT NULL
  - `total_capacity`: INTEGER NOT NULL (CHECK total_capacity > 0)
  - `seats_booked`: INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 (CHECK seats_booked >= 0)
  - `base_fare`: REAL NOT NULL (CHECK base_fare > 0)
  - `current_fare`: REAL NOT NULL (CHECK current_fare > 0)
  - `min_fare`: REAL NOT NULL (CHECK min_fare > 0)
  - `max_fare`: REAL NOT NULL (CHECK max_fare >= min_fare)
  - `status`: VARCHAR(20) DEFAULT 'SCHEDULED'
  - `evidence_quality_status`: VARCHAR(30) DEFAULT 'Strong data'
  - `evidence_quality_details`: TEXT (JSON serialized audit dictionary)
  - `last_updated`: DATETIME DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP

8.2. Table: `recommendations`
  - `id`: INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT
  - `trip_id`: INTEGER NOT NULL (FOREIGN KEY -> trips.id)
  - `demand_category`: VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL ('LOW', 'MEDIUM', 'HIGH')
  - `current_fare`: REAL NOT NULL
  - `proposed_fare`: REAL NOT NULL
  - `fare_adjustment_pct`: REAL NOT NULL
  - `confidence_score`: REAL NOT NULL
  - `rationale`: TEXT NOT NULL
  - `status`: VARCHAR(20) DEFAULT 'PENDING' ('PENDING', 'APPROVED', 'REJECTED', 'OVERRIDDEN', 'BLOCKED')
  - `evidence_quality_status`: VARCHAR(30) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'Strong data'
  - `evidence_quality_details`: TEXT NOT NULL (JSON serialized 7-point audit dictionary)
  - `created_at`: DATETIME DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP

8.3. Table: `operator_decisions`
  - `id`: INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT
  - `recommendation_id`: INTEGER NOT NULL (FOREIGN KEY -> recommendations.id)
  - `trip_id`: INTEGER NOT NULL (FOREIGN KEY -> trips.id)
  - `operator_id`: VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL
  - `operator_name`: VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL
  - `decision`: VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL ('APPROVED', 'REJECTED', 'OVERRIDDEN')
  - `previous_fare`: REAL NOT NULL
  - `new_fare`: REAL NOT NULL
  - `fare_delta`: REAL NOT NULL
  - `reason_notes`: TEXT
  - `created_at`: DATETIME DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP

8.4. Table: `system_settings`
  - `id`: INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT
  - `key`: VARCHAR(50) UNIQUE NOT NULL
  - `value`: TEXT NOT NULL
  - `description`: TEXT
  - `updated_at`: DATETIME DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP

11. ASSUMPTIONS, KNOWN BOUNDARIES & FUTURE ROADMAP
================================================================================

11.1. Operational Assumptions:
  - Synthetic Data: Baseline demo database uses synthetic routes based on popular
    Indian intercity corridors (e.g., Bengaluru - Hyderabad, Chennai - Coimbatore).
  - Deterministic Pricing: Algorithmic recommendations are governed by explicit
    mathematical business policies rather than self-modifying statistical weights.
  - Single Operator Environment: In prototype mode, operator identity is captured
    via selection/session input rather than enterprise SSO.

11.2. Known System Boundaries:
  - The prototype does not execute real-time GPS telemetry ingestion or live bus
    tracking; location is derived from scheduled route timetables.
  - Fare changes approved in the dashboard update internal fleet pricing records;
    distribution to consumer booking channels requires a dedicated GDS/OTA connector.

11.3. Future Roadmap (Phase 4 & Beyond):
  - Milestone 4.1: Direct Integration with live TBS FleetOS Ticketing APIs.
  - Milestone 4.2: Historical Machine Learning Demand Model trained on seasonal
