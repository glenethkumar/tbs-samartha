3. THE 5-MINUTE COMPREHENSIVE JURY WALKTHROUGH
================================================================================

PART 1: THE CORE PROBLEM & MARKET CONTEXT (1 Minute)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Intercity bus transport in India is massive (over 1.5 million daily passenger
  trips across private operators), but operates on razor-thin operating margins.
- Two major revenue leakages occur daily:
  * Off-peak spoil: Buses running under 30% occupancy lose ₹15,000–₹25,000 per
    trip in unrecoverable perishability.
  * Peak-time spill: High-demand routes sell out too early at standard rates.
- Why off-the-shelf dynamic pricing fails for buses:
  * Bus operators are traditional business owners. They need total control.
  * Routes have government/operator tariff caps (minimum and maximum fares).
  * Data from booking counters can be delayed or incomplete.

PART 2: ARCHITECTURE & INNOVATIONS (1.5 Minutes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FleetYield AI introduces four core engineering innovations:

1. Unified Rule-Based Demand Classifier:
   - Replaces unpredictable ML weights with transparent, configurable business rules:
     * Low Demand (<30% occupancy): -10% promotional step to accelerate volume.
     * Medium Demand (30% to 70% occupancy): 0% adjustment, preserves baseline.
     * High Demand (>70% occupancy): +15% yield optimization on remaining scarce seats.

2. Explainable Pricing Confidence & Evidence Quality Framework:
   - Categorizes every trip into one of three deterministic evidence tiers:
     * Strong Data: Fresh data (<=120 mins), valid pace window (>=2h). High confidence.
     * Limited Data: Computable, but historical window is brief. Warns operator.
     * Insufficient Data: Data is stale (>120 mins) or invalid. Triggers an explicit
       BLOCKED status to protect operational safety.
   - Provides a 7-point audit drawer: Current occupancy, booking pace, departure
     countdown, data age, input blockers, policy rules, and min/max clamps.

3. Human-in-the-Loop Decision Ledger:
   - Operators can Approve, Reject, or Manually Override any recommendation.
   - Overrides require reason notes, are bounded by trip min/max constraints, and
     are committed to an immutable audit log.

4. Fixed-Fare Baseline Simulator with Underperformance Detection:
   - Directly compares dynamic yield against a static baseline under 4 elasticity presets.
   - Crucially, it mathematically highlights when dynamic pricing underperforms
     (e.g., Inelastic Markets where discounting seats loses revenue without gaining
     volume), proving honesty over marketing hype.

PART 3: DEMONSTRATION & TRACTION (2 Minutes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Walk the jury through the live UI (refer to Section 4 below).
- Demonstrate the live transition from trip inventory -> recommendation audit ->
  operator approval -> audit log -> baseline simulation.

PART 4: SUMMARY & ECOSYSTEM FIT (30 Seconds)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- FleetYield AI integrates natively into TBS FleetOS.
- It transforms TBS from a ticketing aggregator into an indispensable operational
  operating system for fleet owners, boosting operator retention and yield.

5.1. FOR TECHNICAL JUDGES (Architecture, Reliability & Code Quality)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Key Points to Emphasize:
- Decoupled Clean Architecture:
  * FastAPI backend with strict Pydantic v2 data contracts.
  * React 18 frontend with strict TypeScript types (`types.ts`) matching backend models.
  * SQLite persistence (`fleetyield.db`) using relational schema constraints
    (`CHECK min_fare <= max_fare`, `CHECK total_capacity > 0`).
- Deterministic Pricing Policies:
  * Pure, isolated mathematical functions in `pricing_policy.py`. Zero side effects.
  * Nearest ₹10 commercial rounding (`round(fare / 10) * 10`) with strict clamp guards.
- Comprehensive Pre-Flight Validation Engine (`validation_service.py`):
  * Guards against negative fares, inverted boundaries, overbooked capacity, and
    stale fixtures (>120 mins).
- Automated Regression Test Suite:
  * 37 automated tests in `backend/tests/` running via Pytest (100% pass rate in ~2s).
  * Covers unit boundaries, currency rounding, invalid input rejections, simulation
    non-negativity, duplicate decision prevention, and API contracts.

6. ANTICIPATED JURY QUESTIONS & WINNING ANSWERS (DEFENSE CHEAT SHEET)
================================================================================

Q1: "Why did you build a rule-based engine instead of training a Machine Learning
     model like XGBoost or a Neural Network?"
A1: "That was an intentional, critical engineering and product choice:
     1. Operator Trust: Bus operators reject black-box models that cannot explain
        why a fare changed. In our system, every adjustment is 100% explainable.
     2. Regulatory Compliance: Transport authorities require auditable tariff rules.
        A deterministic rule engine can be defended in an audit; an ML model cannot.
     3. Cold-Start Problem: New routes or post-pandemic schedule changes lack
        training data. Deterministic rule policies work reliably from day one.
     4. Roadmap: In Phase 4, we plan to use ML as a supplementary demand fore-
        casting signal, but the core pricing policy will always retain deterministic
        safety clamps."

Q2: "What happens if a bus operator has bad internet or sends outdated booking data?"
A2: "We built an explicit Data Freshness Engine. If the last data update is older
     than 120 minutes, the system marks the evidence quality as 'Insufficient Data'
     and automatically assigns a BLOCKED status. The algorithm refuses to calculate
     a recommendation, preserves the current fare safely, and alerts the operator.
     We never make pricing recommendations on stale data."

Q3: "How do you ensure you don't overcharge passengers during high-demand rushes?"
A3: "Every trip record has an immutable `max_fare` ceiling set by fleet management
     or local regulatory transport brackets. Even if occupancy is 100% and booking
     velocity is surging, the algorithm is mathematically clamped at `max_fare`.
     Furthermore, dynamic pricing in our system is strictly based on capacity and
     time—never on discriminatory user profiling or cookies."

Q4: "Can dynamic pricing actually lose money compared to a standard fixed fare?"
A4: "Yes! And our Baseline Simulator explicitly proves this. In an inelastic market,
     dropping ticket prices doesn't stimulate enough volume to offset the lower fare.
     Unlike commercial tools that claim 'guaranteed +25% profit', FleetYield AI
     explicitly flags when dynamic pricing underperforms the fixed baseline, giving
     operators empirical evidence on which routes should remain on fixed fares."

Q5: "Is this connected to live ticketing right now?"
A5: "In accordance with strict hackathon truthfulness rules, this prototype operates
     in an internal decision-support mode using synthetic demo data. Fares approved
     by the operator update our persistent database and internal decision ledger;
     they do not alter live passenger tickets until integrated with TBS's production
     ticketing webhook APIs. We never pretend an external integration exists when
     it is simulated."

Q6: "How did you test your project?"
A6: "We implemented 37 automated tests in Pytest covering unit boundaries, edge
     cases, zero occupancy, full capacity, inverted bounds, currency rounding,
     database transactions, and API contracts. The entire test suite passes in
     under 2.5 seconds with zero mocks and 100% success rate."
